Eswatini vs. Global Frameworks

Eswatini's legal approach to cybercrime is shaped by both local legislation and international conventions.

Framework Key Provisions
Eswatini Computer Crime & Cybercrime Act, 2022 Comprehensive national legislation covering illegal access, data interference, computer fraud, child pornography, and cyberterrorism. Penalties range from fines of E100,000 to E100 million and imprisonment from 1 to 20 years[reference:5][reference:6].
Budapest Convention (Council of Europe) The first international treaty on cybercrime. Requires signatories to criminalize offenses like illegal access, data interference, and computer-related fraud. Sanctions must be "effective, proportionate, and dissuasive," including deprivation of liberty[reference:7][reference:8].
Malabo Convention (African Union) Adopted in 2014, it requires State Parties to criminalize offenses affecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of ICT systems. It also mandates effective, proportionate, and dissuasive criminal penalties, including fines and imprisonment[reference:9][reference:10].
